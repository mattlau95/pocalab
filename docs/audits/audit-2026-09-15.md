## Portfolio readability — the 90-second reader

**What they arrive with.** The portfolio case study (`portfolio/projects/pocalab.html`) is strong: problem, role, four named decisions with trade-offs, a 45 → 12 minute outcome, and a reflection. Its "Repo" button lands on github.com/mattlau95/pocalab, which shows: description *"Add images to create a printout for photocards."*, homepage **pocalab.vercel.app** (the app lives at pocalab.app), no topics, a file list with two `audit-*.md` files and nine `docs/` files, and a README that is the unmodified Vite template. The repo undoes the case study. It says "scaffold" where the portfolio said "considered product."

**Requires insider context to understand**

- `MAT-395, MAT-396, MAT-397 — Already done` and 90 more ticket references in the devlog (`docs/DEVLOG.md`, 93 lines) and in most commit subjects. Fine in commits; in the devlog they read as noise because the reader has no Linear access.

- Domain terms with no glossary at the front door: photocard, bleed / trim / safe, n-up, long-edge duplex, 2-up. The devlog defines them; the README does not.
- Pre-rename residue: package name `photocard-generator` (`package.json:2`), storage keys `photocard-project` / `photocard-deck` (`src/hooks/useProject.ts:6-7`), homepage `pocalab.vercel.app`. A reader wonders which name is current.
- `audit-2026-06-10.md`, `audit-2026-06-13.md` at the root measure against `ux-fundamentals-checklist.md` and `AUDIT-REPORT-TEMPLATE.md`, neither of which is in the repo. The standard being applied is invisible.
- `docs/project-study.md`, `docs/seo-monetization.md`, `docs/marketing-channels-spike.md`, `docs/feedback-survey.md`: internal planning with titles that say nothing to an outsider.

**Claims a reader can check against the code.** The case study says *"Nothing hardcodes a dimension — every number derives from `dimensions.ts` `mmToPx()` or `pdf.ts` `mmToPt()`."* In the repo, `mmToPt` lives in `src/utils/layout.ts:4`, `src/utils/printLayout.ts:3` defines a second `MM_TO_PT`, `src/utils/cropImage.ts:3-4` redefines `DPI` and `MM_PER_IN` and hardcodes `55` / `85` at lines 43 and 46 using `Math.round` where `dimensions.ts:4` uses `Math.floor`, and `printLayout.ts:18-19, 46-47` hardcodes `85` / `55` again. The case study points the reader straight at the one place the code does not match it. The "crawler lede" is also presented as a decision, and #28 flags it as borderline hidden text; own it in the README or remove it.

**The single most impressive thing in this repo** is the print-fidelity pipeline: a browser-only app that rasterizes a rotated, zoomed crop to exactly 697 × 1051 px at 300 DPI with background and bleed compositing (`src/utils/cropImage.ts:13-83`), lays out n-up sheets in millimetres with a validity check (`src/utils/printLayout.ts:17-43`), mirrors the back sheet for a long-edge duplex flip (`src/utils/printPdf.ts:69`, `src/utils/layout.ts:61-64`), and draws crop marks, so a card comes out of a home printer at 55 × 85 mm with a registered back. The devlog explains why that is hard better than the code does: *"The catch — why it's not trivial"* at `docs/DEVLOG.md:31-43`, followed by the measured 45 → 12 minute result at line 49.

**Would a 90-second reader find it?** No. It sits at line 31 of a file inside `docs/`, behind a template README, and the four utility files that implement it have no index or comment tying them together. The reader will spend their 90 seconds on the Vite README, wonder about the two audit files, open `package.json` and see `photocard-generator`, and leave. The case study is the only place the pipeline is explained, and the repo is where they go to verify it.
